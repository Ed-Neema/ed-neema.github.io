About

Most AI safety work asks whether a model will refuse a harmful request. I am interested in a quieter failure: an agent given a harmless task that deletes a database, wipes a drive, or rewrites files nobody asked it to touch. I want agents whose actions stay proportionate to what was asked, and evidence that shows when they do not.

I also work on medical AI. Language models give health advice at enormous scale with no system to record when it goes wrong, and speech models can mishear a drug name or dose while their overall error rate barely moves. I design reporting systems, datasets and evaluations that make those failures visible.

Research

  1. 2025 to now

    Proportional action in tool using agents

    How agents behave under ambiguity, risk and uncertainty, and inference time controls that keep their actions scoped to the task. I am building a benchmark that measures the changes an agent makes that the task did not require, scored by how reversible and how disproportionate they are.

    A reporting system for medical harms from LLM advice

    A proposed system for recording and analysing harms linked to medical advice from general purpose language models, combining a harm taxonomy, a tiered privacy model, a data architecture and an analysis of the regulatory gap.

  3. 2026 to now

    BantuMed

    A medical speech dataset and benchmark in Kinyarwanda, Swahili, Kirundi and Luganda, built from simulated clinician and patient consultations. It separates overall accuracy from errors that change clinical meaning, such as medication names, doses, symptoms and negation.

    medical NLPspeechdatasets

  4. 2026

    Does retrieval help LLMs diagnose?

    A controlled comparison of five retrieval setups on 60 MedQA cardiology questions. Hybrid retrieval gave the largest gain, single methods often hurt, and the reranker mainly guarded against bad retrievals. The differences were not yet significant at this sample size.
